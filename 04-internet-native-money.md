# The internet's native currency, unpacked

*Foundation document. "Bitcoin is the native currency of the internet" bundles several different claims. This page separates them. Last updated November 10, 2026.*

## Why this page

The phrase is used as if it were one idea. Its best-known use is hedged: in 2018, Jack Dorsey said "The internet is going to have a native currency… I don't know if it will be bitcoin but I hope it will be" ([CNBC](https://amp.cnbc.com/amp/2018/05/16/twitter-ceo-wants-bitcoin-to-be-the-internets-native-currency.html)). In 2021 he called bitcoin the "best candidate" ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/112299/bitcoin-big-part-twitter-future-ceo-jack-dorsey)), and Square's CFO said bitcoin "has the opportunity to be the strongest contender" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2021/10/14/square-cfo-bitcoin-is-the-best-bet-for-a-native-currency-of-the-internet)). Every primary use found came from Dorsey or Square/Block.

The idea is older than bitcoin. The bitcoin white paper builds on "a proof-of-work system similar to Adam Back's Hashcash" ([Bitcoin white paper](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf)), which was proposed in 1997 to throttle email spam ([Hashcash](http://www.hashcash.org/papers/hashcash.pdf)). This page separates the claims inside the phrase, so each can be checked on its own.

## Six claims

| Claim | What would have to be true | Evidence so far | Status | Where in this library |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Paying instead of ads** | People pay small amounts per article, song or video, replacing advertising | Thirty years of attempts that closed or pivoted; what worked is bundled, prepaid or voluntary (below) | Mostly failed with people as payers | Value-for-value podcasting in [Earning in bitcoin](12-earning-in-bitcoin.md) |
| **Pricing spam** | A small cost per message makes spam uneconomic | Proposed since 1992; never the default for email; used today mostly as computing work, not money (below) | Niche | This page |
| **Machine payments** | Software pays software per request, using the web's "402 Payment Required" | Real but small, and 98.6% in USDC | Growing, mostly in stablecoins | [Agentic payments](13-agentic-payments.md) |
| **Paying for AI crawling** | AI companies pay publishers when crawling or using content | Pilots and licensing deals; billed by card, bank or stablecoin (below) | Early | This page |
| **Permissionless receiving** | Anyone online can be paid, without an account or approval | Strongest when people are cut off; episodic | Real, narrow | [Money that can't be frozen](15-money-that-cant-be-frozen.md), [Community-driven economies](11-community-driven-economies.md) |
| **Unit of account** | Prices online are set in bitcoin | Almost none | Out of scope: it's about what money is, not about demand for payments | — |

## Thirty years of paying instead of ads

### The attempts

| Attempt | Years | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DigiCash (eCash) | 1990s | Its only US bank trial ended in 1998; pilots worldwide reached about 30,000 active accounts and 300 merchants before DigiCash went bankrupt ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/digicash-idea-finds-new-life-in-more-flexible-ecash)) |
| CyberCash, First Virtual | 1990s | One moved into card processing, the other left payments; "the public never clamored for anonymous transactions" or micropayments ([ESJ](https://esj.com/Articles/1999/03/10/Boom-then-Bust-How-Electronic-Cash-Faltered.aspx), 1999) |
| Millicent (DEC) | 1990s | Prepaid "scrip" for small payments; in hindsight, publishers chose ads, not readers ([DSHR](https://blog.dshr.org/2021/09/millicent.html)) |
| Flattr | 2010–2023 | A monthly budget split among the sites users liked; waiving its fee "did not help" ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/twitter-halts-swedish-company-flattrs-social-payments)); shut down in 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flattr)) |
| Blendle | 2014–2019 | Pay per article; ended per-article sales in 2019 because subscriptions were "a much more stable source of income": "quarters per article are not going to make the difference" ([Nieman Lab](https://niemanlab.org/2019/06/micropayments-for-news-pioneer-blendle-is-pivoting-from-micropayments/)) |
| Google Contributor | 2014–2017 | A monthly payment to see fewer ads; accounts closed and balances refunded in 2017 ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2017/01/17/google-contributor-closed/)) |
| Coil and Web Monetization | 2018–2023 | Streaming payments to sites; Coil ceased operations in 2023 ([Interledger Foundation](https://community.interledger.org/interledger/changes-to-the-web-monetization-ecosystem-discussion-post-1emc)) |
| Brave and BAT | 2017– | 130.8 million monthly users; creators earn a share of *advertising* revenue, so BAT funds ads rather than replacing them ([Brave](https://brave.com/transparency/)) |
| A Lightning-paid blog | 2018 | About 1¢ to read; nearly 20,000 invoices in seven months; the developer "had to really babysit the node" ([CoinDesk via Yahoo](https://au.finance.yahoo.com/news/lightning-powered-blog-sees-20-083041247.html)) |

### Why they failed

- **Deciding costs more than paying.** Nick Szabo: "The user interface and the cognition of the user thus remain the bottleneck to transaction granularity" ([Szabo](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/rob/Courses/InformationInSpeech/CDROM/Literature/LOTwinterschool2006/szabo.best.vwh.net/micropayments.html), late 1990s). Clay Shirky's 2000 essay put it bluntly: "Users hate them" (quoted by [DSHR](https://blog.dshr.org/2021/09/millicent.html)).
- **People prefer flat rates.** Andrew Odlyzko: "consumers are willing to pay more for flat-rate plans than for metered ones," and sellers gain by bundling; "Micropayments are likely to continue disappointing their advocates" ([Odlyzko](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/case.against.micropayments.txt), 2003).
- **Free is special.** In experiments, people treat a zero price as if it "adds to its benefits," not just lowers the cost ([Shampanier, Mazar and Ariely](https://people.duke.edu/~dandan/webfiles/PapersPI/Zero%20as%20a%20Special%20Price.pdf), 2007). The gap between free and one cent is the largest of all ([Hargadon, quoting Kopelman](https://andrewhargadon.com/2007/03/10/the-first-penny/)).

### What worked instead

| Model | Scale | How it avoids the per-item decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscriptions and memberships | Patreon about $2 billion a year to creators ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/08/07/patreon-kajabi-10-billion-creator-economy-payouts-funding-since-launch/amp/)); Substack over 5 million paid subscriptions ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/12/substack-five-million-paid-subscribers-journalist-reporter-newsletter/)) | One decision a month |
| App stores and in-game currencies | $131 billion in digital goods and services on Apple's App Store in 2024 ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/au/newsroom/2025/06/global-app-store-helps-developers-reach-new-heights/), Apple-commissioned); Roblox bookings of $6.8 billion in 2025 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/roblox-revenue-up-36-yy-as-grow-a-garden-and-steal-a-brainrot-drive-multiple-milestones/)) | A prepaid balance, held by a platform |
| Tips and stickers | YouTube's Super Thanks "between $2 and $50" ([YouTube](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/introducing-newest-member-supers-family-super-thanks/)); Kakao's emoticon creators earned about 700 billion won ([KED Global](https://www.kedglobal.com/newsView/ked202111290014?lang=us)) | Paying is voluntary and expressive, not a gate |
| Value-for-value podcasting | $127,900 from listeners to creators on Fountain in 2024 ([Stacker News](https://stacker.news/items/829867)) | Voluntary streaming and boosts |

**What it shows:** payments that look like micropayments succeed when a platform holds a prepaid balance, when purchases are bundled, or when paying is a form of expression rather than a toll. Pay-per-item gates for people have failed for thirty years, regardless of the rail. See [Payments that feel different](16-payments-that-feel-different.md).

## Pricing spam

- **The idea:** make the sender "compute a moderately hard, but not intractable, function" before a message is accepted ([Dwork and Naor](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/pricing-via-processing-or-combatting-junk-mail/), 1992). Hashcash proposed the same for email in 1997; Microsoft Research's Penny Black project explored refundable postage, where wanted mail costs nearly nothing ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=144959), 2003).
- **The objection:** to make spam unprofitable, every sender would be capped at about 1,750 messages a day, and with hijacked machines the cap falls to 250, which "would now inconvenience 1% or 13% of legitimate email users" ([Laurie and Clayton](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rnc1/proofwork.pdf), 2004). A later paper argued proof of work can work combined with sender reputation ([Liu and Camp](https://ideas.repec.org/p/net/wpaper/0618.html), 2006).
- **Paid email was tried, and dropped.** Goodmail charged senders about a quarter cent per message for guaranteed delivery at AOL and Yahoo; the EFF called it "a 'pay to speak' email measure" ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/node/59426), 2006), Yahoo dropped it in 2010, and Goodmail shut down in 2011, having "struggled to find a market" ([Word to the Wise](https://wordtothewise.com/2011/02/goodmail-shutting-down)). Facebook tested $1 to reach a stranger's inbox in 2012 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2012/12/21/facebook_paid_message_delivery/)); X tested $1 a year for new accounts in 2023 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2023/10/17/x-not-a-bot-program)), and paying didn't stop verified bots: "paid verification alone isn't a solution" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/10/it-sure-looks-like-x-twitter-has-a-verified-bot-problem/)). None published results.
- **What mail providers chose instead:** rules, not postage. Since 2024, Gmail requires bulk senders to authenticate and keep spam complaints "below 0.30%" ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Spam was still about 45% of email in 2025 ([Kaspersky](https://securelist.com/spam-and-phishing-report-2025/118785/)).
- **Where pricing is used today, it's mostly computing work.** Tor's onion services switch on proof-of-work puzzles under attack ([Tor](https://blog.torproject.org/introducing-proof-of-work-defense-for-onion-services/), 2023); Cloudflare's Turnstile includes "proof-of-work tests" ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/turnstile-ga/)); Anubis puts proof of work in front of websites to block AI scrapers, adopted by GNOME, FFmpeg and UNESCO ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/the-open-source-software-saving-the-internet-from-ai-bot-scrapers/)). Its authors call it "a bit of a nuclear response," since it also blocks archivers ([Anubis](https://github.com/TecharoHQ/anubis)).
- **In bitcoin communities, money is used.** Stacker News charges sats for posts and comments "to prevent spam and to encourage quality contributions," multiplying the fee for rapid repeats ([Stacker News FAQ](https://github.com/stackernews/stacker.news/blob/master/docs/user/faq.md)). Nostr's specifications let relays charge fees ([NIP-11](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/11.md)) and describe zaps and proof of work as spam deterrents ([NIP-57](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/57.md), [NIP-13](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/13.md)). One paid relay also accepts cards ([nostr.wine](https://nostr.wine/)). None publishes how much spam fell.

**What it shows:** charging alone hasn't stopped spam anywhere it was measured. Where it's used, it's one signal among several, usually invisible, and usually computing work rather than money. Bitcoin communities are the main place money is used, without published results.

## AI breaks the ad model

The case for micropayments was always weakest where people decide each payment. AI agents and crawlers change that: they don't get tired of deciding, and they don't see ads.

- **Agents don't click.** Cloudflare's CEO: "the problem is agents don't click on ads" ([Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/podcast/cloudflare-needs-100m-tps-from-crypto-to-fix-the-internet), May 2026). When Google showed an AI summary, users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits, against 15% without one, and clicked a link inside the summary in 1% ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), 2025). Google search traffic to over 2,500 news sites fell 33% in a year ([Reuters Institute](https://www.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/news-publishers-expect-search-traffic-fall-more-40-next-three-years-new-risj-report-finds), January 2026).
- **Crawling far outpaces referrals.** In July 2025, Cloudflare counted about 38,000 pages crawled by Anthropic per visit referred back to a news site, 1,091 for OpenAI and 5.4 for Google, on its news customers' sites ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/crawlers-click-ai-bots-training/)). These ratios vary widely between reports and windows ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/everyone-is-quoting-the-same-number-and-getting-different-answers/588659/)).
- **Paying for crawling.** Cloudflare's pay per crawl answers crawlers with "402 Payment Required" and a price, and acts as merchant of record ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-pay-per-crawl/), 2025); it's still in closed beta, billing crawlers daily by card or bank account ([Cloudflare docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/features/pay-per-crawl/what-is-pay-per-crawl/), [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/x402/)). In July 2026 it began evolving into payment per use of content ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/cloudflares-new-policy-pushes-ai-companies-to-pay-for-publishers-content/)). Its Monetization Gateway, in closed beta, settles in stablecoins over x402, starting with USDC ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/30/cloudflare-tool-businesses-ai-agents-stablecoins/)). Cloudflare announced its own stablecoin, NET Dollar, in 2025 ([Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/press/press-releases/2025/cloudflare-introduces-net-dollar-to-support-a-new-business-model-for-the-ai-driven-internet/)) *[to verify: no launch confirmed]*.
- **Others.** TollBit asks unverified AI bots to pay for access, free for websites ([TollBit](https://tollbit.com/blog/fastly-partnership/)). The RSL standard, backed by Yahoo, Ziff Davis, O'Reilly, Stack Overflow, Cloudflare and Akamai, lets sites publish licensing terms for AI use ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/rsl-ai-licensing-10-now-an-official-industry-standard,259144), December 2025). Large publishers sign licensing contracts instead: Amazon reportedly pays the New York Times $20–25 million a year ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/the-new-york-times-and-amazons-ai-licensing-deal-is-reportedly-worth-up-to-25-million-per-year-135523853/)).
- **Which rail.** None of these schemes uses bitcoin or Lightning in the sources found. Asked about the rail, Cloudflare's CEO said: "it's gotta be some sort of stable coin solution" ([Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/podcast/cloudflare-needs-100m-tps-from-crypto-to-fix-the-internet)).

**What it shows:** the strongest new argument for internet-native payments comes from AI, where the payer is a machine and the ad model breaks. The infrastructure being built for it runs on cards, bank accounts and stablecoins.

## Throughput, honestly

- **The scale Cloudflare describes.** Cloudflare handles "about half a billion, 500 million transactions per second"; between 1% and 10% might be paid for, so "between 5 million and 50 million transactions per second," and "day one, I need 10 million." The 100 million figure was an invitation: anyone who can build a blockchain for "100 million transactions per second" should "call us" ([Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/podcast/cloudflare-needs-100m-tps-from-crypto-to-fix-the-internet)). These are estimates, not measurements.
- **Visa, for comparison.** Visa processed 258 billion transactions in fiscal 2025 ([Visa 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316125000089/v-20250930.htm)), about 8,200 a second on average *[our arithmetic]*. Its last published capacity figure is "more than 65,000 transaction messages a second," from 2017 ([Visa](https://jp.review.visa.com/dam/VCOM/download/corporate/media/visanet-technology/visa-net-fact-sheet.pdf)).
- **Lightning has no single throughput number.** Payments move through channels without settling each one on-chain, so capacity depends on how many channels and nodes carry payments. Each channel in LND allows up to 483 payments in flight at once ([Lightning Labs docs](https://docs.lightning.engineering/the-lightning-network/multihop-payments/hash-time-lock-contract-htlc)); the public network had about 3,794 BTC of capacity across 32,676 channels and 16,237 nodes in August 2026 ([mempool.space](https://mempool.space/api/v1/lightning/statistics/latest)). No source publishes a measured network-wide rate.
- **Stablecoin chains.** Tempo claims sub-second finality without publishing a rate ([Tempo](https://tempo.xyz/developers)); one report puts its actual use at about 2.5 transactions a second ([Protos](https://protos.com/?p=122462), April 2026). Solana is working toward 1 million a second ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/382411/jump-cryptos-firedancer-hits-solana-mainnet-as-the-network-aims-to-unlock-1-million-tps)).

**What it shows:** nothing today processes payments at the rate Cloudflare describes. Any system that gets there will likely net or batch payments between a few large parties rather than settle each one, and that's true whichever currency it uses. *[assumption]*

## Who is competing for the role

| | Stablecoins (x402, USDC) | Cards | Prepaid credits and API keys | Bitcoin over Lightning (L402) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Who can freeze** | The issuer, by law ([Dollars and bitcoin](14-dollars-and-bitcoin.md)) | Issuer and network | The platform | Nobody, if self-custodied; custodians can |
| **Smallest practical payment** | Fractions of a cent | About $0.30 fixed fee | Any, deducted from a balance | Fractions of a cent |
| **Account needed** | A wallet; exchanges check identity | A bank and card | An account with each seller | A wallet; Lightning needs liquidity |
| **Backed by** | Coinbase, Cloudflare, Google, Visa, Stripe (x402 Foundation) | Visa, Mastercard | Every API seller today | Lightning Labs, Alby, bitcoin companies |
| **Used today for machine payments** | 98.6% of on-chain agent payments | Pilots | Most AI and API spending *[assumption]* | About 1–2% of paid endpoints; no published volume |

See [Agentic payments](13-agentic-payments.md) for the figures.

## Open questions

1. Will publishers' crawl payments run on a currency at all, or on netted invoices between a few platforms and AI companies?
2. Do bitcoin communities that price posts with sats see less spam, and could that be measured?
3. Is there any per-item payment for people that has worked at scale without a platform holding a prepaid balance?
4. What would Lightning need to carry machine payments at the scale Cloudflare describes, and who would run it?
5. Does "native" matter to buyers and sellers, or only cost, speed and who can freeze?
