# Group orders: research kit

*A plan and materials for the first wave of direct research in area 11: interviews ("say"), money-flow mapping ("make") and following live orders ("do"). Ready to run. Last updated November 8, 2026.*

## Goals

1. Understand why managers and joiners use the tools and payment methods they do.
2. Map where money, trust and information move in a group order, and where they break.
3. Rank the pains for managers and for joiners, separately.
4. Learn whether any manager would try a different payment step in a real order.

**Hypotheses to test** (from the teardown, [Group orders: a teardown of what managers built](11a-group-orders-teardown.md)):

- Managers' biggest burden is bookkeeping (matching payments, tracking status, chasing deadlines), not the payment itself.
- Joiners accept Friends & Family risk because fees feel worse than the risk, not because they don't care about protection.
- Cross-border joiners pay noticeably more and are sometimes excluded.
- Manager burnout is a main reason orders and accounts end.

## Who

| Group | How many | Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Group order managers | 5 | Ran at least 3 orders in the last 12 months; at least one with international joiners |
| Joiners | 10 | Joined at least 2 orders in the last 12 months; at least 4 joining from another country than the manager's |
| Former managers | 1–2 (optional) | Stopped in the last 3 years; to learn why |

**Adults only (18+).** Many fans are teenagers. Excluding them keeps consent simple and avoids any question of collecting data from minors.

**Mix:** at least two countries for managers (for example the US and one in Southeast Asia or Europe), and a spread of fandoms. Korean-language managers can be a second wave, with a translator.

## Recruiting

- **X:** managers announce orders with hashtags (`#[name]GOs`, "GO" plus the group name). Reply or DM with a short, friendly ask.
- **Reddit:** r/kpopforsale and r/kpopcollections.
- **Discord** servers for specific fandoms, with moderators' permission.
- **Snowballing:** managers know other managers (one manager in the teardown got their Korean address from another manager).
- **Incentive:** a gift card or a contribution to the next fan project of their choice, roughly $30–50 for an hour. *[Decide amount and currency.]*

**Sample message:**
> Hi! I'm a designer researching how group orders work: how you collect payments, track orders and handle shipping across countries. Would you be up for a 45–60 minute video call? I'd thank you with [incentive]. Nothing to sell, and I won't share anything that could identify you.

Don't mention bitcoin in recruiting. It biases who replies and what they say.

## Interview guide: managers (45–60 minutes)

**Warm-up (5 min)**
- How did you start running group orders? How many have you run?
- Which groups or items, and where are your joiners?

**Walk through the last order (20 min)**, asking them to share their screen and show the actual form, spreadsheet and messages where possible:
- From announcing it to the last package going out, what happened, step by step?
- Where did the money go at each stage, and in which currency?
- How did you know who had paid what? What went wrong?
- How long did the admin take?

**Payments (10 min)**
- Why do you ask for [Friends & Family / e-Transfer / other]?
- Have you lost money on an order? What happened?
- Have you had to cover someone's share? For how long?
- How do international joiners pay you? What does it cost them?

**Trust and reputation (5 min)**
- How do new joiners decide to trust you?
- Have you had disputes or scams? How were they resolved?

**Wrap-up (5 min)**
- If one part of running orders disappeared, which would you pick?
- What would make you stop running orders?
- If a tool handled [their biggest pain], would you try it in a real order?

## Interview guide: joiners (30–45 minutes)

- How did you start joining group orders? How many have you joined, and from where?
- Walk me through your last one, from finding it to receiving it.
- How do you decide whether to trust a manager?
- Have you lost money or items? What happened?
- How do you pay, and what does it cost you, including conversion?
- How do you keep track of what you've paid and what's still coming?
- What's the most frustrating part? What's the best part?
- How do you feel about Friends & Family payments? Would you pay more for protection?

## Money-flow mapping (make, 30 minutes, in or after the interview)

Use a shared whiteboard (FigJam, Miro or Excalidraw), or paper over video.

1. **Draw the people:** the manager in the middle, then joiners, the seller in Korea, the Korean address or forwarder, shipping companies, PayPal or the bank.
2. **Draw the money:** an arrow for every payment, labeled with amount, currency, method and when it happens.
3. **Draw the information:** forms, spreadsheets, emails, posts, DMs.
4. **Mark the pain:** red stickers where things break or take time, green where they work well.
5. **Ideal version:** "If you could redesign this from scratch, what would it look like?"

Photograph or export each map. Compare them afterwards for shared patterns.

## Following live orders (do, 2–3 orders)

Ask 2–3 managers if you can follow one order from announcement to delivery, from public posts and a short check-in at each payment stage.

Record per stage: dates, number of joiners, late or wrong payments, questions received, drop-outs, total admin time (estimated by the manager), currency and fees.

## Consent and data

- Explain the purpose, that participation is voluntary, and that they can stop at any time.
- Ask before recording. Store recordings privately and delete them after analysis.
- Use pseudonyms in all notes and write-ups. Don't publish handles, screenshots with names, or anything that identifies a manager or joiner without explicit permission.
- No minors.

## Analysis

- **Per interview:** a one-page summary (who, process, pains, quotes, surprises).
- **Across interviews:** cluster pains with sticky notes or affinity mapping, separately for managers and joiners. Rank them by how often they appear and how much they cost (money, time, trust).
- **Check the hypotheses:** for each, supported, contradicted or unclear, with evidence.
- **Output:** a short public write-up of findings, with a money-flow map of a typical order.
