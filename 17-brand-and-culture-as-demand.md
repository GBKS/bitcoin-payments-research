# Brand and culture as demand

*Deep dive, first draft. Builds on the November 8 quick scan, and looks across payments and other industries. Desk research only. Last updated November 10, 2026.*

## Thesis

Bitcoin's culture celebrates buying and holding, and its image puts many people off; Spiral's report makes the same point. The evidence below, from payments and from other industries, suggests what brand and culture actually do for demand:

- **Visibility is the best-supported mechanism.** Independent research on social networks, word of mouth and solar panels finds that products people can *see* others using spread further, especially when the benefit is visible too.
- **Fintechs that grew through culture grew through word of mouth,** often paid for with referral bonuses. Big giveaways and ad campaigns bought spikes, not lasting use.
- **Bitcoin payments are invisible.** There's no card to ask about, no feed, and no shareable artifact. Its visible culture is about price, and its owners are concentrated among young men. Ownership in the US is falling.
- **Payment use is pulled by the recipient.** Among Americans who paid with crypto, the most common reason was that the person or business being paid preferred it.

**The hypothesis:** this may be an opening rather than a dead end. A visible, shareable artifact on the *recipient's* side, such as a payment name, a request link, a gift or a card people ask about, could do for bitcoin payments what the $cashtag did for Cash App, provided it shows a benefit and doesn't expose private details. Brand works as a lever inside another area (community economies, gifting, earning), not as a market on its own.

*Status: a working hypothesis from desk research. Most growth figures here are companies' own claims; the independent evidence is about mechanisms (visibility, word of mouth), not about bitcoin.*

## What we mean

**Brand and culture as demand:** people adopt a payment product because of what it says about them, what they see others doing, or what it lets them express, not only because of its features or price.

| Mechanism | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visibility | Others can see the product being used | Monzo's coral card, Apple's white earbuds |
| Shareable artifacts | Something people post that advertises the product | Wordle's grid, Spotify Wrapped, Cash App $cashtags |
| Word of mouth and referrals | People recommend it, sometimes for a bonus | Nubank, Monzo, Revolut |
| Characters and design | Familiar characters or designs on a financial product | KakaoBank's Kakao Friends cards |
| Rituals and games | A seasonal moment with a payment at its center | WeChat red envelopes, Alipay's Fu cards ([Payments that feel different](16-payments-that-feel-different.md)) |
| Giveaways and campaigns | Money or attention spent to get sign-ups | PayPay, LINE Pay, crypto Super Bowl ads |
| Presence in culture | Music, sport, memes | Cash App in hip-hop |

## What people say, and what they do

**Say:**

- People outside bitcoin don't say they dislike it; they say it isn't relevant to them. Spiral's 2022 survey of 1,030 people in nine countries (Argentina, Peru, Colombia, Greece, Indonesia, Nigeria, the Philippines, Portugal, Turkey), plus 41 interviews, found women and men view bitcoin's safety similarly. Women cited a lack of clear learning resources, bitcoin rarely coming up in their social circles, and a focus on price over practical use: "How can bitcoin help me buy milk at the store?" Relatable voices mattered, and over a third preferred local influencers ([Spiral](https://spiral.xyz/how-women-across-the-world-view-bitcoin.pdf)). *[to verify: the PDF returned 404 on November 10, 2026; check an archived copy, including the number of interviews]*
- In the US, the most common reason non-owners give is not knowing enough; over half cite distrust, fear of losses or risk ([SEC Office of the Investor Advocate](https://www.sec.gov/files/experiences-crypto-assets-us-population.pdf), July 2026, 5,550 respondents).
- In surveys, people rank social features last when choosing a payment app, after speed and fees ([Motley Fool Money](https://www.fool.com/money/research/paypal-venmo-zelle-survey), 2,000 US adults).

**Do:**

- **Few people pay with crypto, and the recipient decides.** In 2025, 10% of US adults used crypto, but only 2% used it to buy something, pay or send money. Among them, the most cited reason was that "the person or business receiving the money preferred cryptocurrency" (26%), ahead of speed (19%), privacy (17%) and cost (14%) ([Federal Reserve, SHED 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2025-report-economic-well-being-us-households-202605.pdf), May 2026).
- **Ownership is falling and narrow.** Among US investors, 11% owned crypto in June 2026, down from 17% a year earlier; men aged 18 to 49 remain the most likely owners, at 24% ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/714590/crypto-ownership-falls-2025-high.aspx), September 2026). Of US adults who have ever used crypto, 27% of men and 11% of women ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/06/08/about-1-in-5-americans-have-used-crypto-republicans-use-has-ticked-up/), June 2026). 72.8% of non-owners say there's no chance they'll buy in the next six months ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/experiences-crypto-assets-us-population.pdf)).
- **Expression gets used anyway.** About 30% of approved Venmo transactions in a 2016 sample carried emoji, by Venmo's own count ([Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/whats-the-most-expensive-emoji-according-to-venmo)). In a study of Venmo's social feed (two surveys and 14 interviews), most people found seeing friends' payments awkward, yet 11 of 14 interviewees kept the public default ([ACM CSCW on Medium](https://medium.com/acm-cscw/friends-dont-need-receipts-the-joys-and-risks-of-venmo-s-social-feed-1e8cb6724b91)).

**Say versus do:** fintech brands *say* they win on features; what they *did* was become part of how people express themselves. People *say* social features don't matter and *do* use emoji and keep feeds public. Non-owners *say* they need to learn more; most aren't waiting to.

## Why bitcoin payments struggle here

- **Nothing to see.** A Lightning payment leaves no visible trace: no card at the counter, no feed, no artifact to post. Rogers' diffusion research names observability, "the degree to which the outcomes of an innovation are visible to others," as one of the attributes that speed adoption ([Newcastle University theory library](https://open.ncl.ac.uk/theories/8/pdf/diffusion-of-innovations)).
- **The visible culture is about price.** Super Bowl ads, stadium names and memes advertise buying and holding, not paying.
- **The image is narrow.** Ownership skews toward young men, and confidence is low: 63% of Americans have little or no confidence that crypto is safe and reliable ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/10/24/majority-of-americans-arent-confident-in-the-safety-and-reliability-of-cryptocurrency/), 2024).
- **Visibility has a privacy cost.** Of 389 million Venmo transactions studied, 10.5% of notes leaked sensitive information such as health, politics or drug use ([PoPETs 2022](https://petsymposium.org/popets/2022/popets-2022-0069.php)). Self-custodial users often care about privacy more than most.

## Case studies

### Cash App: a payment name in songs

Hip-hop artists name-dropped Cash App in songs and videos, which Jack Dorsey said the company hadn't expected. Cash App Fridays let users reply with their $cashtag for $100–$500 prizes (an estimated $60,000 in 2019), and artists ran similar giveaways to their own followings at low cost. Monthly active users grew from 7 million in 2017 to 15 million in 2018 and 24 million in 2019, faster than Venmo ([Trapital](https://www.trapital.com/memos/how-hip-hop-helped-cash-app-grow-faster), 2020). By September 2025, Cash App had 58 million monthly actives, with growth now credited to "marketing initiatives, giveaways, and influencer partnerships" ([Block](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1512673/000119312525269715/d48712dex991.htm)).

**What it shows:** the $cashtag was a payment address that worked as a public identity: short, shareable, and visible in culture. The giveaways were cheap because the artifact did the advertising.

### Word of mouth as the growth engine

- **Nubank** acquired "approximately 80%-90% of our customers organically on average per year since our inception," at a cost of about $5 per customer in 2021; its purple card was the brand ([Nubank F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691493/000119312521314359/d213207df1.htm)).
- **Monzo's** coral debit card prompted people to ask "what card is that?" ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/monzo-marketing-strategy/)). In its 2025 annual report, "67% of customers join through word of mouth," with over 12 million customers ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/annual-report/2025)). By the 2026 financial year the share was 79%, but Monzo also spent about £29.5 million on referral bonuses, roughly three times the year before ([FStech](https://www.fstech.co.uk/fst/Monzo_Spends_Tens_Of_Millions_On_Referral_Bonuses_In_Bid_For_New_Customers.php)).
- **Revolut:** 65% of new customers in 2024 came organically or by referral ([a16z](https://www.a16z.news/p/the-algorithm-that-keeps-compounding)). **Chime:** member referrals have been "the single largest channel" of growth since 2022 ([Chime S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025025059/chimefinancialinc-sx1wq1da.htm)).

**What it shows:** a product people talk about grows cheaply, and "word of mouth" often includes paid referrals. All figures are the companies' own.

### Characters and rituals: Korea and China

- **KakaoBank** opened over 100,000 accounts in its first eight hours in 2017, and its Kakao Friends debit cards sold out ([KEIA](https://keia.org/analysis/koreas-new-craving-for-kakao-bank/)). It passed 20 million users by 2022, about 40% of Korea's population ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/business/banking-finance/20221109/kakaobank-draws-over-20-mil-users-after-opening-in-2017)), and over 31 million character cards had been issued by 2024, though they also pay 0.2% cashback ([Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025011409235600117)). Launching inside the KakaoTalk messenger is a confound.
- **WeChat's red envelopes** at the 2015 Spring Festival Gala drew 11 billion shakes from users in 185 countries ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2015/02/27/wechat-payment-clear-winner-in-tencent-alibaba-digital-lucky-money-war/)). **Alipay's Fu cards** game in 2017 saw 168 million users complete the set; its aim was "to increase the sociability of its app" ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2017/03/11/china-hongbao-alipay-real-innovator)).

**What it shows:** characters and rituals can move enormous numbers, but each case rode on an existing messenger or super-app with hundreds of millions of users.

### Giveaways and campaigns: spikes that fade

- **Japan.** PayPay reached 10 million users about ten months after launch, helped by a ¥10 billion giveaway that ran out in ten days ([PayPay](https://about.paypay.ne.jp/en/pr/20190808/01/)). Its rival LINE Pay ran a ¥30 billion reward campaign ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/08/08/paypay-10m-users/)) and closed in April 2025, folding into PayPay ([LY Corp](https://www.lycorp.co.jp/en/news/release/008632/)).
- **India.** Paytm sponsored cricket and grew fast during the 2016 demonetization, but by August 2025 held 7% of UPI transactions, far behind PhonePe and Google Pay ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/august-marks-all-time-high-upi-transactions-for-phonepe-and-google-pay-10065606)). Regulatory action against Paytm in 2024 is a confound.
- **Crypto's 2022 Super Bowl.** Coinbase's QR-code ad lifted its app from 186th to 2nd in the US App Store ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/134101/crypto-apps-soar-in-popularity-after-super-bowl-splurge)). The following quarter, Coinbase reported "significant improvements in our brand awareness" while monthly transacting users fell 19% ([Coinbase](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1679788/000167978822000047/finalsecversionq120225-1.htm)). A year later, crypto advertisers had "zero representation" at the Super Bowl ([AP via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/ap-national-business/2023/02/10/year-after-crypto-bowl-crypto-ads-vanish-from-big-game-2/)).
- **Bitcoin acceptance as marketing.** Steak 'n Shake reports double-digit same-store sales growth since accepting bitcoin in May 2025, but hasn't disclosed what share of orders are paid in bitcoin ([Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/steak-n-shake-says-sales-grew), September 2026). *Self-reported and confounded.*

**What it shows:** money and attention buy sign-ups. What follows the spike decides whether they stay.

### Lessons from other industries

- **Visibility spreads use, research shows.** In a randomized experiment on Facebook, automatic, passive broadcasts of a user's actions produced a 246% increase in peer adoption; personal invitations added a further 98%, but broadcasts generated "more total peer adoption" because they're used more often ([MIT Sloan on Aral and Walker](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/?p=12601)). Products that are more publicly visible get talked about more, both right away and over time ([Berger and Schwartz](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/BzzAgent.pdf), 2011). Solar panels visible from the street raise neighbors' adoption, moderated by "the economic value that the peers receive" ([Bollinger et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:41:y:2022:i:3:p:453-476), 2022).
- **Users often invent the shareable artifact.** Wordle's colored grid was created by a player and then built into the game; it made the game visible to people who didn't play, and players grew from 90 to 300,000 in about two months ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success), [MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2022/01/31/npr-the-new-york-times-buys-wordle)).
- **Hype fades and doesn't travel.** Liquid Death left the UK with £2 million a year in sales ([Food Manufacture](https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2025/02/10/liquid-death-exiting-uk-less-than-2-years-since-debut)); Supreme sold for about $1.5 billion four years after being bought for about $2.1 billion ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/vf-sells-supreme-essilorluxottica/721639/)); Oatly's North American volumes fell 10.9% in early 2025 ([Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/oatly-plant-based-milk-demand-US-europe-coffee-gen-z-marketing-earnings/747127/)).
- **Entry through a trusted place.** Oatly entered the US through coffee shops and baristas rather than grocery shelves ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/01/oatly-enters-starbucks-cafes-nationwide-as-it-prepares-for-a-big-year.html)), the equivalent of a merchant or community that introduces a payment.

**What it shows:** culture amplifies a product people already like and can see working. It doesn't sustain demand on its own.

### Leading with the payment, not the asset

Several products hide bitcoin entirely: Cash App users pay Square sellers over Lightning "using dollars from your Cash App balance, no bitcoin required" ([Block](https://block.xyz/bitcoin/roadmap/item/pay-dollars-over-lightning-025)); Tando and MoneyBadger deliver local money ([Last-mile infrastructure](18-last-mile-infrastructure.md)). No source tests whether hiding the word "bitcoin" raises use.

## Key assumptions

1. **Visibility would help bitcoin payments spread.** Supported by research in other fields; untested for payments.
2. **The recipient is the right place for a visible artifact.** Supported by the Federal Reserve's finding that recipients drive crypto payments, and by the $cashtag. Untested for bitcoin.
3. **Visibility can be designed without exposing private details.** Partly supported: Kakao envelopes, WeChat covers and card art express without broadcasting amounts. Venmo shows the cost of getting it wrong.
4. **Brand works only on top of a product people already value.** Supported by every fading case.

## What could serve this

**Kinds of product**

This area is mostly about practice rather than products:

- **Visible payment identities:** short, memorable names that double as payment addresses (Lightning addresses, BIP-353 names), shareable the way $cashtags were.
- **Shareable artifacts** that show the benefit without revealing amounts or people, the way Wordle's grid hid the answers.
- **Visible objects:** wallet passes, stickers, cards, merchandise.
- **Campaigns and collaborations** with artists, designers and communities, with characters where a community already has them.
- **Tools for creators** to make themes or branded payment pages.
- **Community programs:** forums, ambassadors, local events, merchants who introduce the payment.

## Trade-offs for self-custodial, client-side products

- **It needs no custody or servers.** Brand, themes, visible objects (cards, passes, stickers) and collaborations all happen at the product and communication level.
- **A payment name needs a resolver:** Lightning addresses need a web server; BIP-353 names need DNS. Both are visible identities, with the privacy that implies ([Last-mile infrastructure](18-last-mile-infrastructure.md)).
- **It's a lever, not a market.** Brand creates demand *within* an audience; it doesn't define one. It works best combined with another area (community economies, gifting, earning).
- **Distribution costs money:** Cash App had giveaways and Square's reach; Monzo had a physical card; KakaoBank had KakaoTalk. Most self-custodial wallets have no card and little marketing budget, so collaborations, communities and artifacts that spread themselves are the realistic channels.
- **"Lead with the payment, not the asset"** fits wallets that don't put bitcoin jargon first.

## Risks and open questions

**Risks**

- **Brand without product:** culture amplifies a product people already like; it can't replace one.
- **Authenticity:** communities reject brands that borrow their culture without belonging to it.
- **Privacy:** social, visible payments (the Venmo feed) carry real privacy risks, which matter more for self-custodial users.
- **Regulation of crypto advertising:** the UK's Advertising Standards Authority upheld complaints that a 2025 Coinbase campaign implied crypto solves cost-of-living problems ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/cb-payments-ltd-g25-1305562-cb-payments-ltd.html)).
- **Spikes mistaken for demand:** giveaways and ads produce sign-ups that fade.

**Open questions**

1. Which bitcoin products have grown through culture rather than through bitcoin channels, if any?
2. What's the equivalent of Monzo's coral card or the $cashtag for a self-custodial wallet with no physical card: a payment name, a wallet pass, a gift, a shareable receipt?
3. Which communities would welcome a collaboration, and what would they want out of it?
4. Does "get paid in bitcoin" (Spiral's framing) resonate more than "save" or "spend" with people outside bitcoin?
5. How do visible, social payments work for people who value privacy?

## Research plan: say, do, make

| Method | Kind | Who | What we learn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Artifact audit | Do | What bitcoin users already share publicly about payments (zaps, receipts, payment names, stickers) | Which artifacts people invent on their own, the Wordle lesson |
| Concept tests | Make | 15–20 people outside bitcoin, shown three shareable artifacts (a payment name, a gift, a receipt) | Which one they'd share, and what it makes them think of bitcoin |
| Community collaboration | Make | One community from [Community-driven economies](11-community-driven-economies.md), co-designing a themed payment or gift | Whether a collaboration changes use, not just interest |
| Interviews | Say | 10 people who stopped using a crypto app | What the image did and didn't do |

**Outputs:** a catalog of shareable payment artifacts, one tested collaboration, and an updated thesis.
